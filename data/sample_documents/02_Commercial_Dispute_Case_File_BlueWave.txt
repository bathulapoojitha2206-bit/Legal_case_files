2. Brief Facts
The plaintiff and defendant entered into an EPC contract dated 5 January 2023 for construction of a 40,000 sq. ft. 
warehouse facility at Patancheru, Hyderabad, for a total contract value of 8,60,00,000, with a stipulated completion date ₹
of 4 January 2024. The contract contained a liquidated damages clause (Clause 14.2) providing for deduction of 0.5% of 
contract value per week of delay, capped at 10% of contract value.
Construction was delayed substantially. As of the stipulated completion date, only approximately 62% of the work stood 
completed. The defendant attributes the delay to force majeure (extended monsoon disruption and a cement shortage in the 
region), while the plaintiff contends that the defendant failed to mobilize adequate manpower and materials from the 
outset, and that the force majeure clause (Clause 18) was not properly invoked within the notice period required under the 
contract. The plaintiff terminated the contract on 20 February 2024 and engaged an alternate contractor to complete the 
balance work, incurring additional cost.

5. Key Legal Issues
• Whether the force majeure notice dated 11 November 2023 was issued within the time and form required under 
Clause 18.3 of the contract
• Whether the delay is attributable to force majeure events, contractor default, or a combination of both, and in what 
proportion
• Enforceability and computation of the liquidated damages clause (Clause 14.2) as against actual proven loss
• Validity of the termination and entitlement to recover the additional cost of completion through the substitute 
contractor
• Maintainability of the defendant's counterclaim for unpaid running bills in the same proceeding

9. Relevant Contractual and Legal Provisions
• Clause 14.2 of the EPC Contract — Liquidated damages at 0.5% of contract value per week of delay, capped at 10% 
of contract value
• Clause 18 of the EPC Contract — Force majeure, including notice requirement within 7 days of the triggering event
• Section 73, Indian Contract Act, 1872 — Compensation for loss or damage caused by breach of contract
• Section 74, Indian Contract Act, 1872 — Reasonable compensation where a sum is named as liquidated damages, not 
exceeding the amount stipulated
• Commercial Courts Act, 2015 — Procedural framework governing case management and timelines in the present suit
10. Precedents Relied Upon
The following categories of precedent are being relied upon; specific citations to be finalized by the handling advocate

11. Risk Assessment
The plaintiff's position is aided by the apparently defective and delayed force majeure notice, and by the independent 
expert's report attributing the bulk of the delay to the defendant's mobilization failures. The principal risk is the liquidated 
damages cap under Clause 14.2, which may limit recovery notwithstanding a higher quantum of actual proven loss; 
arguments on recovering damages beyond the cap will need to be carefully framed under Section 74 of the Contract Act.
