2. Brief Facts
The claimant was employed with the respondent as a Senior QA Engineer from 3 June 2019 until his termination on 30 
September 2023. The respondent states the termination formed part of a broader workforce reduction affecting 22 
employees across the QA function, necessitated by the loss of a major client contract, and that one month's notice pay 
along with retrenchment compensation calculated under Section 25F of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 was tendered to 
the claimant at the time of termination.
The claimant contends that the termination was in substance a targeted removal disguised as retrenchment, pointing to the 
fact that two employees junior to him in the same role were retained, in alleged violation of the 'last-in-first-out' principle 
ordinarily expected in retrenchment absent a valid reason for deviation. The claimant further alleges that no prior warning 
or performance-related communication was ever issued to him, and that the real reason for his selection was a workplace 
complaint he had raised in March 2023 regarding a reporting manager, which the respondent denies is connected to the 
termination.

5. Key Legal Issues
• Whether the termination constitutes 'retrenchment' within the meaning of Section 2(oo) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 
1947, and whether Section 25F conditions were duly complied with
• Whether deviation from the 'last-in-first-out' principle under Section 25G was justified by the specialized certification 
rationale advanced by the respondent
• Whether the termination was, in substance, victimization connected to the claimant's internal complaint of March 
2023
• If termination is found invalid, whether reinstatement with back wages or monetary compensation in lieu is the 
appropriate relief

9. Relevant Legal Provisions
• Section 2(oo), Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 — Definition of 'retrenchment'
• Section 25F, Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 — Conditions precedent to retrenchment, including notice and 
compensation
• Section 25G, Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 — Procedure for retrenchment, including the 'last-in-first-out' principle 
absent reasons to the contrary
• Section 25N, Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 — Additional conditions applicable where the establishment falls within 
its scope (not applicable here given headcount)
10. Precedents Relied Upon
The following categories of precedent are being relied upon; specific citations to be finalized by the handling advocate 
ahead of final arguments:
• Authorities on permissible deviation from the seniority principle under Section 25G where genuine and demonstrable 
business reasons, such as specialized skill retention, are shown
• Authorities distinguishing bona fide retrenchment for economic reasons from disguised or colorable termination 
amounting to victimization
• Authorities on the evidentiary burden on a workman alleging victimization to show a causal link, as opposed to mere 
temporal proximity, between a protected act and termination
12. Risk Assessment
The respondent's documentary compliance with Section 25F (notice and compensation) is not seriously disputed. The 
principal litigation risk is the roughly four-month gap between the claimant's internal complaint and his selection for 
retrenchment, combined with the retention of two junior colleagues, which the claimant will argue together suggest 
victimization rather than a neutral business decision. The specialized certification rationale (Ex. M1) and the 
independently documented grievance closure (Ex. M3) are the respondent's strongest counters to this inference.
